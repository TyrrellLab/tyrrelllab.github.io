# Product

Register: brand. This site must persuade the reader and give an identity. It
does not control a task.

## Users

**Primary: possible trainees.** Graduate students, rotation students and
postdoctoral researchers who must decide where to apply. Usually they use a
phone, and they have four other lab sites open. They try to answer three
questions in approximately 90 seconds. Is the science interesting? Can I stay
here for five years? Is there a position for me? They read the People page most
carefully, because they look for a person who is related to them.

**Secondary: collaborators.** Other laboratories in aging, immunology and
vascular biology. They come for one specified capability, they look for it one
time on the Research page or the Publications page, then they leave. They do
not read long text.

Funding bodies, the press, donors and possible patients are real users with a
lower priority. The site satisfies them if it satisfies the two groups above.

## Purpose

This site is the public face of the Tyrrell Lab at the UAB Heersink School of
Medicine. The scientific position of the lab is a true difference from other
laboratories, and the site is built on it. 99% of murine disease models use
young animals. Almost all chronic disease occurs late in life. This lab works
with old mice. The site must show that claim, which is more important than the
visual treatment.

The site is successful if a rotation student says that the website was the
cause of their email.

## Personality

Confident, specified and simple. The voice gives findings and then stops: "Most
chronic disease arrives late in life, yet most models stay young. We work in
old mice." Do not be indefinite. Do not use institutional introductions. Use an
exclamation mark only in true lab news. The register is editorial, not
institutional. The emotional target is believable ambition, not warmth,
authority or excitement.

## Designs to keep away from

- **The default university template.** Stock photographs of persons on stairs,
  blue-grey boxes, three equal feature cards, a carousel that no one clicks.
  This is the most probable failure, because each equivalent lab site looks
  like this.
- **The usual SaaS landing page.** A hero metric, gradient text, a card grid
  with an icon above each heading, glass panels.
- **Brutalist or aggressive design.** Raw HTML aesthetics, hard monospace type,
  severe contrast. The register is not correct for a medical school laboratory.
- **The portfolio with too much design.** Scroll-jacking, very large
  transitions, movement that competes with the science.

## Principles

1. **Give the claim before the decoration.** Each page must reach its point
   before it shows its effects.
2. **Be editorial, not institutional.** The site must read like a good journal,
   not like a CMS.
3. **Answer the trainee's question.** Each page must help a possible student to
   decide whether to send an email.
4. **Make each kilobyte and each frame necessary.** The user with a phone is
   the primary reader.
5. **Use accuracy as personality.** Loose type scales, old figures and
   inaccurate words do more damage to the science than a simple design.

## Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA is the minimum, and the site obeys it. The site also obeys WCAG 2.2
target size (2.5.8). DESIGN.md holds the full contract. Keep it true.

Some readers do not have English as their first language. Thus the measure and
the line height are more important than density.
